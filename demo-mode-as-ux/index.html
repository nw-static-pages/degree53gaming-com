Demo mode is a UX feature, not a marketing one

Free play is the only way a person can learn how a game feels before they pay for it, and that makes it an onboarding feature, not a promotional one. Most products treat demo mode as a checkbox — a "Play for fun" link under the tile — and then wonder why first real-money sessions end in confusion.

Last reviewed: 21 September 2026 · Author: Rory Finch

What a demo teaches that a paytable cannot

Reading a paytable tells you what pays. Playing fifty demo spins tells you the rhythm: how often anything happens, how long the dry runs are, what the bonus looks like, whether the cascade mechanic is fun or just slow. Volatility is a feeling before it is a number, and the demo is where the feeling is learned.

That has a direct product consequence. Players who have demoed a game stake more deliberately, complain less about "rigged" outcomes, and choose games that match their appetite — all of which are good for the player and, over time, for the operator. A demo is a rehearsal, and rehearsals reduce the shock of the performance.

The constraint in Great Britain

Since 7 May 2019, social responsibility code 3.2.11 has required a GB licensee to verify a customer's age before that customer can access any free-to-play version of a gambling game the licensee offers. The Commission's reasoning was that free-to-play games on an operator's site are "tester products" for the real-money versions. Open demos on the public lobby — the norm in most other markets — are therefore not an option for a GB brand.

Design teams usually read that as "demos are dead for the UK". It actually means the demo has to move: from the pre-registration lobby into the post-verification experience, where it can do its real job. A verified player who has not yet deposited is the perfect demo audience — they are inside the product, they are curious, and nothing is at stake yet.

Where the demo belongs in the flow

  1. Directly after verification, as the default state of any game tile: "Try" first, "Play" second. Money is the upgrade, not the entry point.
  2. Inside the game client, as a mode switch, so that a player can flip to demo when a new feature confuses them without leaving the game.
  3. On every new-release card, because "new" is precisely when nobody knows the rhythm yet.

Outside Great Britain, where open demos are allowed, the same logic applies with one addition: demo play on the public site is the best acquisition content a brand has, because it is the product itself. Catalogues built around free play — demo first, details second — tend to hold visitors far longer than review-style pages, for the obvious reason that they are giving people the thing rather than describing it.

Designing the demo-to-real transition

The moment a player switches from demo credits to real money is the highest-friction step in the product, and most implementations make it worse:

  • Do not reset the game. If a player has been demoing at £1 a spin with a particular bet configuration, real mode should open with the same configuration, not the default.
  • Make the balance change unmistakable. Demo balances that look like real balances are the source of the "where did my money go" complaints. Different colour, different label, a short banner: "Demo credits — not real money".
  • Do not fake the RTP. Demo mode must run the same maths as real mode. A demo that pays generously and a real game that does not is the fastest way to a "rigged" reputation, and in a regulated market it is a compliance problem, not just a UX one.
  • Show the switch cost. If switching requires a deposit, say so before the tap, not after.

What to measure

Not "demo sessions" — that number goes up when the tile is bigger and means nothing. Measure: share of first real-money sessions preceded by a demo of the same game; length of first real session with and without a prior demo; and the rate of support contacts about "the game didn't do what I expected" in each group. If the demo is doing its job, the first two go up and the third goes down.

Quick answers

Should demo spins be limited? No. A player who wants to spin a demo for an hour is learning; the product loses nothing.

Should demo mode show the same info screen? Yes, including RTP and volatility. It is the least stressful moment to read it.

Is demo mode a responsible-gambling tool? Partly. It lowers the "I didn't understand the game" category of harm. It does not address the others.

Source for the rule: Gambling Commission, changes to the LCCP on age and identity verification — SR code 3.2.11 (in force 7 May 2019). Observations on behaviour are from project work and are not a published study.
